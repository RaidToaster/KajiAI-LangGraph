I. INTRODUCTION
Automated fact-checking requires more than assigning a
plausibly correct label. A system should identify evidence,
connect that evidence to the claim, disclose uncertainty and
preserve enough provenance for inspection. FEVER estab-
lished an evidence-oriented task using claims derived from
Wikipedia [1]. AVeriTeC extends this setting to real-world
claims and web evidence [2]. Its shared-task score combines
verdictcorrectnesswithevidencequality[3],emphasizingthat
labelagreementaloneisanincompleteaccountofverification.
Retrieval-augmented generation provides external informa-
tion to a language model [4], but retrieved documents, cita-
tions and repeated reasoning do not themselves guarantee an
evidence-supported verdict. Citation evaluation distinguishes
answer correctness from citation quality [5]. This distinction
motivates an architecture in which prediction and acceptance
are observable, separate decisions.
We present KajiAI, an auditable workflow implemented
with LangGraph. A model proposes a factual label and an
evidence assessment. A gate checks retrieved citations, exact
quotations, assertion coverage, source-host diversity and do-
main requirements, followed by automated semantic citation

This paper examines three questions: RQ1, whether the
complete workflow improves label prediction relative to the
same model without retrieval; RQ2, how evidence checks
affect coverage and accuracy among accepted verdicts; and
RQ3, whether additional research improves saved predictions
enough to justify its resource use.
The contributions are an explicit state-and-provenance de-
sign separating prediction from acceptance; a frozen, resum-
able evaluation protocol with failure-inclusive scoring and
fixed-evidence policy replay; and measurements exposing the
interaction between accuracy, selective acceptance and itera-
tion cost. LangGraph is the orchestration mechanism, rather
than a proposed new learning algorithm. The experiment does
notisolateaframeworkmigrationorestablishsuperiorityover
other fact-checking systems.
II. RELATEDWORK
A. Evidence-Based Fact-Checking
FEVER pairs supported and refuted claims with annotated
evidence sentences [1]. AVeriTeC uses real-world claims,
question–answer evidence and textual justifications from web
sources [2]. The AVeriTeC shared task evaluates evidence
retrieval and veracity prediction jointly [3]. We use its de-
velopment labels for a local four-class study; our scores are
not official AVeriTeC scores and cannot be directly compared
with shared-task rankings.
The 2025 AVeriTeC shared task instead specifies open-
weight models, a precompiled evidence store, a single-GPU
resourceconstraintandaone-minuteprocessinglimit[6].Our
cloud-assisted,contemporary-webprotocoldoesnotreproduce
those conditions.
B. Retrieval and Citation Evaluation
Lewis et al. introduced a retrieval-augmented formulation
combining parametric generation with an external document
memory [4]. Self-RAG learns retrieval and critique behaviors
usingreflectiontokens[7].KajiAIdoesnottrainsuchamodel:
its research loop uses structured assessments, query templates
andexplicitroutingrules.Thismakesthebehaviorinspectable

C. Selective Prediction
Selective classification introduces a reject option to trade
coverage for accuracy on accepted instances [10]. KajiAI
applies this perspective through evidence requirements rather
than a learned selective classifier. We report coverage and
conditional label accuracy without importing statistical risk
guaranteesfromselective-learningmethods.Modelconfidence
categories and citation-review confidence have not been cali-
brated against independent human judgments.

B. Query Construction and Retrieval
At round t, retrieval constructs an ordered list from (i)
follow-upqueriesproposedinthepreviousassessment,(ii)the
claim plus “evidence,” (iii) the claim plus “counterevidence,”
(iv) each assertion plus “evidence,” and (v) each assertion
plus “original source fact check.” It trims and deduplicates
strings, excludes previously used queries and selects the first
two remaining candidates when the budget permits.
Candidate construction therefore combines model sugges-
tionswithhardcodedtemplates.Nolearnedoptimizerchooses
the best query. Jev ranks returned URLs for fetching, a
separate decision from choosing a query. TinyFish supplies
up to five results per search and up to two full-page fetch
attempts per search. Failed fetches leave available snippets in
the ledger.
A source record stores the URL, query, provider, retrieval
time, snippet or page text, content hash and review scores.
Full-page text is preferred to a snippet for the same URL,
truncatedto20,000charactersandhashedaftertruncation.The
canonical record used in an assessment is retained by URL
and hash, permitting replay against the same text version. Jev
sourcereviewestimatesrelevance,evidencevalue,counterevi-
dencevalueandinjectionrisk.Reviewedrecordswithinjection
scoreatleast0.9,orallthreerelevance/evidencescoresbelow
0.1, are excluded from usable evidence. Unreviewed records
retain permissive defaults; this filter is not a demonstrated
injection defense.

D. Bounded Research and Auditability
When gaps remain, routing allows further research if exe-
cutionhasnotfailedandresourceandstagnationlimitspermit
it. Twoconsecutive rounds with no newledger records trigger
a stagnation stop. A new record can be a changed snippet for
an already fetched URL, so ledger growth is not equivalent to
new usable evidence.
Each round retains its raw assessment, exact usable source
versions, gaps, budget snapshot, elapsed time and stop deci-
sion.Reportedtokenusageisaggregated,withacompleteness
flag when accounting is unavailable. Time and token limits
govern scheduling; an in-flight request can finish after the
soft deadline or exceed the reported-token threshold. Missing
usage prevents a guaranteed spend cap. The report preserves
proposed and accepted verdicts separately and distinguishes
insufficient evidence from execution errors.

VI. DISCUSSIONANDLIMITATIONS
A. What the Results Support
The paired comparison offers evidence that the combined
workflow improves label prediction over this control. Accep-
tance replay shows semantic checks removing wrong labels
from a structurally eligible subset while also rejecting correct
labels. Trace analysis identifies a cost-bearing failure mode in
repeated research. These observations support treating predic-
tion, acceptance and resource use as separate outcomes.
TheydonotestablishacausaladvantageofLangGraphover
CrewAI, a hallucination-reduction percentage or benchmark-
leading performance. No independently executed retrieval-
only,Jev-disabledoralternative-frameworkconditionhasbeen
completed. Fixed-assessment replay audits acceptance policy
but cannot replace those experiments.
B. Threats to Validity
Service availability: All planned claim-condition attempts
are complete, including 13 recorded fatal failures. Quota and
concurrent-slot interruptions required resumption across time.
Aposthocsensitivityanalysison487pairswithoutfatalerrors
gives a workflow-minus-control difference of 14.37 points
(bootstrap95% CI:9.45–19.10);remoteresponsesandcurrent
web results can drift. Retaining failures exposes operational
performance, while rerunning failed claims and silently re-
placing them would change the experiment.
Temporal and verdict exposure: Retrieval uses the con-
temporary web without a historical publication cutoff. Later
reports and published verdicts can help predict older claims.
OriginalAVeriTeCannotationsaddresstemporalevidencecon-
cerns [2], but our protocol does not reproduce that constraint.
Pretraining exposure is unknown, and the repeatedly used
development split is not a pristine held-out test.
Evidence validity: Literal checks verify provenance only.
Jevsupportjudgmentslackindependentcalibrationandhuman

entailment labels. Model-reported independence and peer-
review flags can be wrong. Text similarity can miss para-
phrased copying or flag legitimate overlap; snippets can omit
qualifying context. The prepared human audit has not been
performed.
Taskandcontrolscope:Differentpromptsmakethemodel-
only comparison a combined-workflow effect. Heuristic de-
composition can create incomplete fragments. Excluding NEI
from acceptance restricts task coverage. Multilingual, mul-
timodal, adversarial and user-facing behavior has not been
evaluated.
Reproducibility: Hashes preserve source and text, not fu-
ture service responses. Temperature zero does not guarantee
bitwise reproducibility. Soft deadlines, incomplete token ac-
countingandquotasaffectmeasurements.Secretsareexcluded
from archived artifacts.
C. Next Evaluation Steps
Independent human review should assess citation support
and source credibility. A separate study can compare one-
round and iterative workflows under controlled retrieval and
test stopping rules based on usable-evidence novelty rather
than ledger growth. A held-out, temporally constrained eval-
uation and independently executed retrieval-only and Jev-
disabled conditions would better isolate the contribution of
each mechanism. Such changes must be versioned and eval-
uated separately to avoid mixing post hoc improvements into
this condition.
VII. CONCLUSION
KajiAI makes proposed verdicts, evidence acceptance and
research history independently inspectable. In a completed
500-claim AVeriTeC development evaluation, the workflow
improves paired label accuracy over a model-only control,
while a strict gate accepts few claims and later research can
worsen saved predictions. The measured tradeoff concerns
coverage, conditional correctness and cost. Independent cita-
tion validation, held-out evaluation and controlled studies of
revised stopping policies remain necessary.
