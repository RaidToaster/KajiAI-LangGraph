Evidence Gates and Failure-Inclusive Evaluation for
Claim V eriﬁcation with KajiAI
Anonymous review draft
Abstract—KajiAI is a LangGraph workﬂow that separates
proposed factual labels from evidence acceptance and retains
retrieved text versions for audit. We froze a comparison of
the current workﬂow and an evidence-adjudicated candidate
on 500 A V eriTeC development claims with DeepSeek and a
ﬁxed Jev veriﬁer . This interim analysis accounts for 47 of 1,000
scheduled cells, all carried forward from a resource pilot. The
baseline proposed 6 correct labels in 24 cells and accepted one.
The candidate proposed 3 correct labels in 23 cells, had 15
execution failures and accepted zero. Across 23 terminal pairs,
the candidate-minus-baseline proposal agreement difference was
−8.7 percentage points (95% paired bootstrap interval −30.4
to +13.0). The other 450 claims remain untouched in this
comparison. The separate historical Gemma study measured
a 13.6-point gain over a model-only control, and a controlled
stopping challenge forfeited two correct acceptances when it
skipped reassessment. These studies use different conﬁgurations
and answer different questions. Pilot-based model selection, live
retrieval, incomplete coverage and outstanding human citation
adjudication limit inference.

I. I NTRODUCTION
Automated fact-checking requires more than assigning a
plausibly correct label. A system should identify evidence,
connect that evidence to the claim, disclose uncertainty and
preserve enough provenance for inspection. FEVER estab-
lished an evidence-oriented task using claims derived from
Wikipedia [1]. A V eriTeC extends this setting to real-world
claims and web evidence [2]. Its shared-task score combines
verdict correctness with evidence quality [3], emphasizing that
label agreement alone is an incomplete account of veriﬁcation.
Retrieval-augmented generation provides external informa-
tion to a language model [4], but retrieved documents, cita-
tions and repeated reasoning do not themselves guarantee an
evidence-supported verdict. Citation evaluation distinguishes
answer correctness from citation quality [5]. This distinction
motivates an architecture in which prediction and acceptance
are observable, separate decisions.
We present KajiAI, an auditable workﬂow implemented
with LangGraph. A model proposes a factual label and an
evidence assessment. A gate checks retrieved citations, exact
quotations, assertion coverage, source-host diversity and do-
main requirements, followed by automated semantic citation
review. Failed checks preserve the prediction while withhold-
ing an accepted verdict. Unresolved gaps can trigger further
research, subject to limits on rounds, searches, page fetches,
elapsed time and reported tokens.
The frozen DeepSeek comparison asks how an evidence-
adjudicated candidate changes proposal agreement, acceptance
coverage, execution failures and resource use relative to the
standard workﬂow. We report the exposed pilot stratum sep-
arately from the other 450 cases. Historical studies give two
different comparisons: Gemma with and without the workﬂow,
and three cloud models under scripted ﬁxed-evidence stopping.
The evaluation treats operational failure as a result. It
preserves each started cell and the service reservations asso-
ciated with it. Source versions and saved assessments support
inspection of disagreements among label agreement, evidence
checks and resource use.
II. R ELATED WORK
A. Evidence-Based Fact-Checking
FEVER pairs supported and refuted claims with annotated
evidence sentences [1]. A V eriTeC uses real-world claims,
question–answer evidence and textual justiﬁcations from web
sources [2]. The A V eriTeC shared task evaluates evidence
retrieval and veracity prediction jointly [3]. We use its de-
velopment labels for a local four-class study; our scores are
not ofﬁcial A V eriTeC scores and cannot be directly compared
with shared-task rankings.
The 2025 A V eriTeC shared task instead speciﬁes open-
weight models, a precompiled evidence store, a single-GPU
resource constraint and a one-minute processing limit [6]. Our
cloud-assisted, contemporary-web protocol does not reproduce
those conditions.
B. Retrieval and Citation Evaluation
RAG combines parametric generation with external doc-
ument memory [4]; Self-RAG learns retrieval and critique
through reﬂection tokens [7]. KajiAI instead uses structured
assessments, query templates and explicit routing rules, allow-
ing inspection of query generation and stopping behavior.
FIRE integrates retrieval and veriﬁcation iteratively, choos-
ing a ﬁnal judgment or another search query using conﬁdence
[8]. EMULA TE distributes search-result ranking and webpage-
content assessment across agents [9]. Iterative research and
role separation therefore precede KajiAI. Our empirical focus
is preserving source versions, separating provisional prediction
from rule-based acceptance, and measuring changes across
frozen trajectories.
ALCE evaluates generated answers along ﬂuency, correct-
ness and citation quality dimensions [5]. Our literal-quote
check addresses a narrower property: whether a quoted string
occurs in stored text for its cited URL. A separate model

C. Selective Prediction
Selective classiﬁcation trades coverage for accuracy through
rejection [10]. KajiAI selects outputs through evidence re-
quirements; its conﬁdence categories and citation-review
scores lack independent calibration, so the reported coverage
and conditional label accuracy carry no statistical risk guaran-
tee.
III. S YSTEM DESIGN
A. Stateful Graph and Execution
LangGraph supports stateful workﬂows combining deter-
ministic operations and model calls [11]. Eight nodes execute
KajiAI’s process in Fig. 1. The shared state includes the origi-
nal claim, optional reference date, domain, assertions, retrieval
ledger, research history, proposed assessment, evidence gaps,
budgets and stop reason. Individual claims follow a sequential
graph. The frozen DeepSeek comparison uses one worker. The
historical Gemma runner used up to three.
The DeepSeek comparison uses the exact Ollama tag
deepseek-v4.1-flash:cloud through a loopback ser-
vice and checks the returned model ID. TinyFish Search and
Fetch and TypeSafe Jev supply the other network services.
Within one attempt the retriever reuses fetched pages. Each
arm starts a fresh workﬂow state. The coordinator saves
checkpoints at attempt boundaries. The graph itself has no
persistent checkpoint store.
Jev, accessed through the TypeSafe SDK, supplies the work-
ﬂow’s domain, search-ranking, source-review and citation-
support judgments. Domain classiﬁcation uses Jev when its
conﬁdence is at least 0.6, with an Ollama classiﬁer and
then keyword scoring as fallbacks. The proﬁles are general,
scientiﬁc, political and health/medical. The baseline uses a
deterministic conjunction heuristic limited to six fragments.
Fragments act as coverage obligations within the assessment.
The candidate uses neutral research questions as guidance and
retains the original claim as the decision target.
B. Query Construction and Retrieval
At round t, retrieval constructs an ordered list from (i)
follow-up queries proposed in the previous assessment, (ii) the
claim plus “evidence,” (iii) the claim plus “counterevidence,”
(iv) each assertion plus “evidence,” and (v) each assertion
plus “original source fact check.” It trims and deduplicates
strings, excludes previously used queries and selects the ﬁrst
two remaining candidates when the budget permits.
Candidate construction therefore combines model sugges-
tions with hardcoded templates. No learned optimizer chooses
the best query. Jev ranks returned URLs for fetching, a
separate decision from choosing a query. TinyFish supplies
up to ﬁve results per search and up to two full-page fetch
attempts per search. Failed fetches leave available snippets in
the ledger.
A source record stores the URL, query, provider, retrieval
time, snippet or page text, content hash and review scores.
Full-page text is preferred to a snippet for the same URL,
truncated to 20,000 characters and hashed after truncation. The
canonical record used in an assessment is retained by URL
and hash, permitting replay against the same text version. Jev
source review estimates relevance, evidence value, counterevi-
dence value and injection risk. Reviewed records with injection
score at least 0.9, or all three relevance/evidence scores below
0.1, are excluded from usable evidence. Unreviewed records
retain permissive defaults; this ﬁlter is not a demonstrated
injection defense.
C. Prediction and Acceptance
The analyzer returns a structured assessment containing a
verdict, conﬁdence category, ﬁndings, cited URLs, quotations,
assertion resolutions, contradictions, gaps and next queries. It
also reports source independence, counterevidence search and,
where required, peer-reviewed support. These model-reported
ﬂags are policy inputs rather than independently veriﬁed facts.

Structural acceptance requires a decisive verdict and High
conﬁdence; nonempty retrieved citations for every ﬁnding;
literal quotations in canonical source text; complete assertion
coverage; at least two cited hosts; no ﬂagged duplicate lineage
among cited sources; and no unresolved gaps or contradic-

health/medical claims require the peer-review ﬂag. A con-
ﬂicting verdict additionally requires supporting and refuting
ﬁndings. Established disagreement belongs in those ﬁndings
and the summary; the contradictions ﬁeld instead records
unresolved objections to the proposed verdict. Distinct hosts,
text dissimilarity and model ﬂags do not prove actual inde-
pendence.
For each eligible ﬁnding–URL–quotation triple, Jev reviews
the quotation and up to 1,200 surrounding characters on each
side. Semantic acceptance requires a “supports” relation with
conﬁdence at least 0.8 for every citation. Missing or failed
citation checks block acceptance. Review identity includes
ﬁnding, URL, quotation and content hash; ﬁnalization repeats
structural checks and conﬁrms review coverage. This tests quo-
tation support for an attached ﬁnding, not the entire inferential
chain from documents to verdict.
D. Bounded Research and Auditability
When gaps remain, routing allows further research if exe-
cution has not failed and resource and stagnation limits permit
it. Two consecutive rounds with no new ledger records trigger
a stagnation stop. A new record can be a changed snippet for
an already fetched URL, so ledger growth is not equivalent to
new usable evidence.
Each round retains its raw assessment, exact usable source
versions, gaps, budget snapshot, elapsed time and stop deci-
sion. Reported token usage is aggregated, with a completeness
ﬂag when accounting is unavailable. Time and token limits
govern scheduling; an in-ﬂight request can ﬁnish after the
soft deadline or exceed the reported-token threshold. Missing
usage prevents a guaranteed spend cap. The report preserves
proposed and accepted verdicts separately and distinguishes
insufﬁcient evidence from execution errors.
E. Evidence-Adjudicated Candidate
The candidate ﬁrst selects literal passages by ID and pro-
poses a factual class. It evaluates passage stance and checks
source paths separately, using source-bound anchors from a
predecision review. The origin-review head adjudicates the
acceptance materiality of each lead. A material or unresolved
class-undermining issue blocks acceptance. The compiler re-
solves selected IDs to their original retained text and rejects in-
valid bindings. An additional independent-provenance review
is required before acceptance. Source references found during
research can supply a follow-up query within the existing
round budget.

D. Ofﬂine Policy Replay and Citation Audit
Policy replay applies alternative acceptance rules to the
same saved ﬁnal assessments and source versions. We compare
structural-only acceptance with the full gate and semantic
thresholds from 0.5 to 0.95. Replay cannot show how changing
a gate would alter later retrieval or reasoning. Removing
exact-quote requirements also changes eligibility for semantic
review, so it is not an isolated semantic ablation.
Citation measurements count ﬁnding–URL occurrences in
ﬁnal assessments, including repeated URLs across ﬁndings.
A blinded human-audit worksheet contains 454 citation rows
from 44 accepted and 50 sampled rejected claims; labels and
acceptance status are stored separately. Human annotation
ﬁelds remain blank. Automated Jev judgments are measure-
ments rather than independent human ground truth.
V. R ESULTS
A. Interim DeepSeek Comparison
The interim snapshot retains 47 of the 1,000 scheduled
cells. The fresh run is checkpointed before its ﬁrst new
cell because the earlier pilot left fewer than the 12 Fetch
URL slots reserved for a new cell. The other 450 claims
contribute zero terminal cells. Table III uses observed cells
as denominators for agreement and reports terminal counts
against the target. All 24 baseline cells completed. Eight
candidate cells completed and 15 failed. Neither arm timed
out. Baseline acceptance coverage was 1/24, with one correct
acceptance on the unpaired baseline cell. Candidate coverage
was 0/23. Across the 23 terminal pairs, baseline and candidate
correctly proposed 5 and 3 labels. The paired difference was
−8.7 percentage points (95% paired bootstrap interval −30.4
to +13.0). No correct or incorrect acceptance occurred on the
matched pairs.
The reused cells generated 1,536,161 input and 395,590
output tokens on Ollama and 1,496,816 input and 185,349 out-
put tokens on Jev. They reserved 185 searches and 366 Fetch
URLs. Worker time including startup was about 70.4 minutes.
At peak uncached list rates these tokens imply estimated costs
of $0.936 for Ollama and $0.063 for Jev. Provider charges
and cache or reasoning counts remain unavailable where the
response does not report them. The journal separates worker
time from quota waits and calendar time. Fresh usage is zero
at this checkpoint.
B. Historical Gemma Paired Label Prediction
Both conditions assess the same 500 claims: 122 Supported,
305 Refuted, 35 NEI and 38 Conﬂicting labels. Table IV
reports the paired label results, including failed attempts.
Full predictions are correct on 337 claims, compared with
269 for the control. The workﬂow is correct on 117 claims

1.35 × 10−7.

17.1%, compared with 40.0% for the control. Improvement is
therefore not uniform across classes.

VI. D ISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS
A. What the Results Support
The historical Gemma comparison shows higher label accu-
racy for its combined workﬂow than for its model-only control.
The interim DeepSeek pairs have lower candidate proposal
agreement and more candidate failures. The adaptive sample,
wide interval and incomplete coverage limit that comparison.
Semantic replay withholds both correct and incorrect labels;
saved trajectories expose costly reassessment with mixed
prediction changes. Prediction, acceptance and resource use
therefore warrant separate measurement.
The study includes no independently executed retrieval-
only, Jev-disabled or alternative-framework comparison. Its ev-
idence supports neither framework superiority, a hallucination-
reduction percentage nor benchmark-leading performance.
B. Threats to V alidity
Service availability: Quota and concurrent-slot interruptions
required resumption across time. A post hoc sensitivity analy-
sis on 487 pairs without fatal errors gives a workﬂow-minus-
control difference of 14.37 points (bootstrap 95% CI: 9.45–
19.10). Remote responses and current web results can drift.
Temporal and verdict exposure: Retrieval uses the con-
temporary web without a historical publication cutoff. Later
reports and published verdicts can help predict older claims.
Original A V eriTeC annotations address temporal evidence con-
cerns [2], but our protocol does not reproduce that constraint.
Pretraining exposure is unknown, and the repeatedly used
development split is not a pristine held-out test.
Evidence validity: Literal checks verify provenance only.
Jev support judgments lack independent calibration and human
entailment labels. Model-reported independence and peer-
review ﬂags can be wrong. Text similarity can miss para-
phrased copying or ﬂag legitimate overlap; snippets can omit
qualifying context. The prepared human audit has not been
performed.
Task and control scope: Different prompts make the model-
only comparison a combined-workﬂow effect. Heuristic de-
composition can create incomplete fragments. Excluding NEI
from acceptance restricts task coverage. Multilingual, mul-
timodal, adversarial and user-facing behavior has not been
evaluated.
Reproducibility: Hosted model revisions and complete
provider-speciﬁc usage are unavailable for the historical study.
Soft deadlines, quotas and web changes limit repeatability.
The companion artifact documents the historical records and
includes the controlled protocol, authored cases, assessment
harness, saved traces and request ledgers. The DeepSeek
companion includes the case and conﬁguration freeze, journal
hashes and records, a separate attempt and service ledger,
and the ofﬂine scorer and table commands. Historical records
and their provenance remain separate. The artifact permits a
recount without service calls. Provider revisions and live web
state may differ in a new execution.
C. Next Evaluation Steps
Independent human review should assess citation support
and source credibility, retaining separate annotations and ad-
judication for the accepted and rejected strata. An independent,
temporally constrained evaluation could measure naturally
occurring stopping eligibility. Executed retrieval-only and Jev-
disabled conditions would help isolate the contribution of each
mechanism.
VII. C ONCLUSION
KajiAI preserves proposed verdicts, evidence decisions and
research traces as separate records. The historical Gemma
comparison measured higher proposal agreement than its
model-only control, and the controlled stopping challenge
lost correct acceptances when it skipped reassessment. The
interim DeepSeek comparison retains the candidate’s failures
and sparse acceptance without establishing a qualiﬁed im-
provement. A quota checkpoint leaves 953 cells untouched.
Completion requires accounting for those cells under the same
freeze and auditing the resulting evidence and costs.
