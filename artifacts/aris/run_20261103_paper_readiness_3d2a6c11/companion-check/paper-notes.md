## Results and interpretation

- Full workflow: 67.4% label accuracy, macro F1 0.500; model only: 53.8%, macro F1 0.417.
- Paired gain: 13.6 percentage points, 95% paired bootstrap interval 8.6–18.6; exact McNemar p=1.3453e-7. A post hoc sensitivity analysis excludes 13 failed claim-condition pairs: n=487, gain 14.37 points, interval 9.45–19.10.
- The full gate accepts 44/500 outputs (8.8% coverage); 42/44 agree with gold labels (95.5%, Wilson interval 84.9–98.7%). Both accepted errors are gold conflicting claims assigned Refuted. These are label-agreement results, not independent evidence validation.
- Structural-only replay accepts 50, with 45 matching labels. Semantic review withholds three wrong and three correct labels. This is fixed-evidence replay, not a separately executed ablation.
- First-round saved predictions score 68.8%, compared with 67.4% for final predictions. Later research changes 18 wrong predictions to correct and 25 correct predictions to wrong. The final-minus-first interval is -4.0 to +1.2 points and includes zero. A separately run one-round condition could behave differently.
- Contemporary-web exposure is substantial: 208/500 final assessments cite a host matching the gold fact-check host. The post hoc host-match group scores 77.9% versus 50.5% for the control; the unmatched group scores 59.9% versus 56.2%. These groups differ in composition and depend on workflow citations; the unmatched group is not proven contamination-free.
- Citation diagnostics: 2,611 occurrences, 1,914 literal provenance matches and automated reviews, 34 non-supporting review relations and 158 supporting relations below the 0.8 threshold. A provenance mismatch is not automatically a hallucination.

The evaluation uses a repeatedly examined development split, current web access and local four-class label scoring. It is not the official AVeriTeC evidence/QA score, a held-out test, a comparison proving LangGraph better than CrewAI, or a measured hallucination-reduction percentage. Iterative retrieval and agent roles have prior work; the paper acknowledges FIRE and EMULATE.
