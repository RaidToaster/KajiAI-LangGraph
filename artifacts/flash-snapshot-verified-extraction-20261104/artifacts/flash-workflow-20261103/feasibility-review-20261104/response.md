## A measured presentation opportunity

Both revision-8 Bloomberg candidate ledgers contain the same 5,193-character PolitiFact article. It reports the household calculation, the 2.63 household-to-person conversion, and an additional calculation by Matthew Gross at the University of Michigan. The text explicitly describes replication and reports about $6 billion rather than Bruenig's $11.4 billion, with both well below the contemporaneous Bloomberg estimate.

In contrast, the DeepSeek requests 012, 023 and 034 all present the same two windows from that article: its headline/introductory claim and its claim restatement. Neither window contains Gross or the replication. DeepSeek subsequently states that the independent methodology is not visible. This is a direct causal hypothesis linking a particular lossy input transformation to the recorded rejection; it does not depend on assuming that another web search succeeds.

The current `_compact_records` selector ranks 72-word windows, starting every 56 words, by claim/snippet term overlap and retains two per source. Repetition of the claim beats the method account. For this exact article, the original selected starts are word 112 and word 0. The relevant alternative starts at word 504, character interval **[3081, 3569)** in the saved `page_text` (zero based, end exclusive). Its 72 words include:

> at the University of Michigan with data-analyzing experience, helped PolitiFact replicate Bruenig’s math. … Gross found that the bottom 38% had about $6 billion in wealth, rather than Bruenig’s $11.4 billion …

The ellipses above are explanatory quotation shortening, not a proposed citation. The implementation must retain the complete contiguous original window.

This is a candidate opportunity rather than an accepted result. The source describes an additional calculation from the same underlying dataset; independent verification does not mean independent underlying data. Its March 2 report and February 25–28 interviews also postdate the February 19 claim, while explicitly examining the historical comparison. The frozen protocol does not impose a blanket publication-before-claim rule. An audit should assess whether the retrospective calculation establishes the stated historical quantity, including population conversion and methodological qualifications, rather than automatically rejecting it because of its publication date or automatically approving it because of the word “replicate.”
