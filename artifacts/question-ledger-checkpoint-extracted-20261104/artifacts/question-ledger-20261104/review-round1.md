## Findings

1. **Correct the attribution control before live assessment.** The existing `insufficient` claim is “Mayor Imani said that Eastport unemployment fell by 20 percent between 2022 and 2023.” A faithful recording can establish that claim without proving the unemployment statistic. Requiring independent statistics to resolve the speech claim manufactures a gap. Conversely, a recording does not establish the embedded statistic. Freeze two separate targets sharing the same authored packet: (a) the current said-P claim, potentially Supported with appropriate speech evidence and guard-compliant independent verification; (b) “Eastport unemployment fell by 20 percent between 2022 and 2023,” unresolved without measurement evidence. Score attribution coverage separately from embedded truth. Do not give the model both alternative workflow arms in one answer and call that a paired comparison.

2. **The strict-parser failure is real only for this diagnostic contract.** `flash_question_ledger.py:worker` uses `json.loads(text)`. GLM fenced the JSON and therefore failed the predeclared strict screen. `llm.py:_parse_json` strips fences and recovers JSON objects; the offline recount also reports runtime-compatible recovery. Preserve the failed strict result and the separately marked recovery. Another service call to remove fences is unwarranted. This bundled five-plan transport is not an actual single-claim runtime execution.

3. **Generated statements violate the intended planning contract, but do not erase the runtime claim.** GLM's Bloomberg comparison drops “owns more than”; several DeepSeek/GLM statements merely say “the claim states/specifies,” identify a reference date, or refer to a preceding sentence. GLM's detached vote statement does not name the subject/date. Such statements are weaker, redundant, or non-self-contained research obligations. `graph.py:decompose` prepends the original claim, and `gate.py:evidence_gaps` requires an assessment for that exact assertion. Therefore a scope-loss acceptance is not demonstrated. The practical harm is extra required assertion/citation work: for example, a reference date supplied by the caller is not a factual proposition that external evidence must establish. Retain these raw outputs; evaluate direct material propositions rather than treating meta-propositions as useful decomposition. Do not silently rewrite a raw plan and credit the model for the correction.

4. **Questions currently affect retrieval only.** `_unused_queries` puts assessment next queries first, then verification questions, then the whole-claim evidence/counterevidence queries. `analyze` receives required assertions and prior gaps, not the questions or a ledger. Questions can therefore change which sources enter the finite budget; they cannot directly produce a material-component assessment in the current analyzer. Several supporting questions can also consume early query slots before an explicit counterevidence query. This is a routing risk worth measuring, not evidence that counterevidence was skipped in a completed question workflow. DeepSeek has useful Bloomberg measurement/population/time/counterestimate leads; GLM does not explicitly ask for the household-to-person conversion. Neither plan resolves any of those issues.

5. **The ledger is an audit primitive, not a semantic guard.** It checks URL/version identity, contiguous quotation, and literal occurrence of supplied date/origin strings. Dates or origins appearing somewhere in a page do not establish that they apply to the linked passage. Blank metadata is allowed; empty passage rows and unresolved questions can coexist with `literal_references_valid=true`. This is consistent with the code's explicit `semantic_support_verified=false` and `independence_verified=false`, but makes the literal-valid flag unsuitable as component completion or acceptance. Missing components are detected; missing semantic support needs a separate human/model judgment. Search through the runtime confirms there is no current ledger integration. Do not describe the existing runtime as already enforcing component-ledger coverage.

6. **The Bloomberg packet supplies a substantive reason to test a ledger, with clear limits.** The method reply already contains $64.2B, $11.4B, 47.8M households, average household size 2.63, about 125.7M people, and PolitiFact's separately named replication yielding about $6B. A ledger can distinguish the wealth estimate, comparison population/conversion, relevant historical period, and replication's actual evidentiary origin. It can show that $6B versus $11.4B does not itself reverse a comparison with $64.2B. It can distinguish the date Sanders spoke from publication and underlying data dates; the reply's “Feb 19–Mar 2020” wording is not a clean single claim-time scope. It cannot make a household proxy exactly equivalent to a ranking of individuals, supply the economist's absent full methodology, prove a Forbes estimate independently, or turn copied reporting into independent corroboration. The GLM gap about absent counterevidence combines search coverage and missing methodology; these must be judged separately for materiality, not erased as a sentence. The Jacobin citation received Jev 0.79 against the 0.8 policy: the cited passage repeats numbers, but does not by itself establish the entire provenance finding. A ledger must retain and diagnose that failure, not remove the citation or relabel it passed. No accepted anchor exists in these records.
